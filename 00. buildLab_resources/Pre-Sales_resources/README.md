# Pre-sales, product, and technical communication track

## Mission

Turn an ambiguous customer problem into a clear, feasible, testable proposal and hand it to the delivery team without creating false promises. This track connects discovery, requirements, solution framing, demos, proposals, and post-sale learning.

## Six-month progression

| Month | Learn and practice | Product deliverable | Validation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — Understand | Stakeholders, discovery questions, user problems, qualification, note-taking | Product brief, stakeholder map, discovery script | Mentor checks open questions and evidence vs assumption |
| 2 — Define | Requirements, user stories, acceptance criteria, prioritization, scope boundaries | Backlog, personas, acceptance criteria, risk/assumption log | Designer, engineer, QA, and cloud reviewers agree on feasibility |
| 3 — Shape the solution | Value proposition, solution overview, architecture story, demo narrative | One-page solution brief and clickable/demoable vertical slice | Technical reviewer checks that claims match the product |
| 4 — Demonstrate | Discovery-led demos, objection handling, security/reliability questions, accessibility | Demo script, FAQ, proof-of-concept plan | Record a demo; peers score clarity, accuracy, and timing |
| 5 — Propose | Proposal structure, scope, milestones, dependencies, estimates, risks, success measures | Proposal and statement-of-work outline | Delivery team validates effort, risks, and exclusions |
| 6 — Handoff and learn | Handoff, decision logs, release communication, feedback loops, case studies | Final proposal, handoff pack, demo, and case study | Customer-style review and post-demo retrospective |

## Required outputs

- Discovery plan and interview notes.
- Problem statement and stakeholder map.
- User stories with acceptance criteria.
- Scope, assumptions, risks, and non-goals.
- Solution brief and architecture narrative.
- Demo script and objection/FAQ sheet.
- Proposal and delivery handoff checklist.
- Final case study grounded in evidence.

## Quality gate

- Every promise has an owner, assumption, or evidence source.
- Requirements are testable and do not prescribe an implementation unnecessarily.
- Scope excludes what the team cannot responsibly deliver.
- Demo data is safe, realistic, and clearly labeled.
- Estimates include dependencies and uncertainty.
- Security, privacy, accessibility, support, and operational concerns are addressed.
- Handoff includes decisions, open questions, acceptance criteria, and next action.
